Near-Optimality: The Full Picture

Upper and lower bounds nearly match across all regimes of ε

ε Upper Bound (Cost) Lower Bound (Cost) Gap
ε = 0 O(n) — probe everything Ω(n)   [Thm 10] Tight
constant c > 1 O(w · Log(n/w))   [RPE, Thm 1] Ω(w · Log(n/(k*+1)w))   [Thm 13] Tight*
any ε > 0 O(w/ε² · Log(n/w) · log n)   [Thm 6] Ω(w/ε²)   [Thm 14] O(Log · log)

* Tight when k* ≤ (n/w)1−δ for any small constant δ > 0.

ε = 0 is Hopeless

Finding the exact optimum requires reading essentially the entire input. No clever algorithm can avoid Ω(n) probes, even in 1D.

Width w is the Right Parameter

Both upper and lower bounds scale with w, confirming that the dominance width captures the intrinsic difficulty of monotone classification.

1/ε² Dependence is Necessary

The Ω(w/ε²) lower bound shows the quadratic dependence on 1/ε is unavoidable — not an artifact of the algorithm design.

Only Polylog Gap Remains

The remaining gap is O(Log(n/w) · log n). Closing this gap — in either direction — is an open problem.

The "true" complexity of Problem 1 is Θ(w/ε²) up to polylogarithmic factors.
